Practical guides

How is a loan payment calculated?

A standard loan has a constant payment made of an interest part, computed on the outstanding balance, and a principal part. Early on, interest dominates; over time the principal share grows. An extra repayment cuts the principal directly and therefore all future interest.

APR: the true cost of credit

The nominal rate does not tell the whole story. The APR includes arrangement fees and insurance and is computed on the real cash flows. Always compare offers on APR, not only on the advertised rate.

Compound interest and the rule of 72

With compound interest, gains earn gains. The rule of 72 gives a quick estimate: divide 72 by the annual rate to get the years needed to double your money. At 6%, expect about 12 years.

Real return and inflation

An investment earning 5% with 3% inflation grows your purchasing power by only about 1.9%. The real return, net of inflation and tax, is the only figure that matters for long-term projections.

Frequently asked questions

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs locally in your browser. Only your preferences (currency, theme, cookie choice) are stored on your device.

Are the results financial advice?

No. They are educational estimates based on the assumptions you enter. For a decision, consult your bank or a licensed adviser.

Where do the displayed policy rates come from?

From central banks' official websites, with the verification date. They change at each monetary policy meeting: check the official source before deciding.

What is the difference between nominal rate and APR?

The nominal rate is the contract rate; the APR adds fees and insurance and expresses the total effective yearly cost of the loan.

Does the calculator handle early repayment?

Yes. Enter a monthly extra payment and a start month: the tool shows the interest saved and the months gained.
